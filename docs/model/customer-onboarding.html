Customer onboarding — two doors, one set of rules

A customer becomes ours in three moves that live on three different clocks: we validate and create (ours), QBO answers with an id (synchronous echo), and ION learns the customer exists (a sync we do not control). The model's core decision is that the waiting is a modelled state, not a null column — and that a task cannot be opened until it ends.

Ruled 2026-08-03 Everything we create flows through the factory, and everything always writes to QBO — but the factory is not the gate for existence. QBO is the leader and customers born directly in QuickBooks must be absorbed, validated, and flagged, never refused. So the rules live in one object (customerFit) that both doors call: the factory refuses on it before creating; the inbound path flags on it after the fact.

The core decision

Three facts forced the shape of this model, and each of them cost something to learn:

  1. The ION customer id does not exist at creation time. ION only learns of a customer when the QBO→ION sync (the ProEdge bridge) runs, and ION exposes no QBO key to look it up by (ADR 006). A promise in memory cannot survive that wait, so the wait is a state on the row.
  2. A service location's identity is its rooftop place id, not its street text. Normalized-street matching is a guess re-derived on every run; a place id is a fact resolved once. When the guessing machinery silently broke, a re-run created 65 duplicate accounts.
  3. Every external write is proven by an echo or a read-back, never by a status code. QBO returns the created Customer with its Id; ION is re-read after the POST.

One value object, used twice — once for QBO, once for ION. The point is that null conflates three different situations that demand three different actions.

Parse, don't validate

A validator returns a boolean and throws away what it learned, so every caller downstream has to trust that somebody checked. A parser returns a more precise type, and the knowledge survives in the type itself: once you hold a Phone, it is a valid phone — the private constructor means there is no way to build one that isn't. Same discipline as Pin.fromTrusted in the routing domain.

The parse result is separate from its consequence, because the consequence differs by door — and that is the whole two-doors rule in one line of code:

DoorEntry pointA parse failure means
Outbound — we are creatingCustomer.draft(input)blocking violations refuse; the customer is never built
Inbound — born in QBOCustomer.rehydrate(id, input, refs)the same failures ride along as violations; we do not get to reject the leader's records
Why Phone is a value object and CustomerShape was not Value-object status is earned by bearing a rule, not by grouping fields. Phone earns it concretely: ADR 006 matches an ION customer to a QBO one on name exact + phone agrees, and agreement across two systems is only decidable if both sides reduce to the same canonical digits — otherwise (973) 943-8251 and 9739438251 silently disagree and every match drops a confidence grade. The old CustomerShape bore no rule; it was a parameter bag, and it also conflated the billing address (a value) with the service address (an entity). Both are gone.
Found by the dry run The first strict parser refused two real customers for listing two phone numbers and three emails — a household, not a defect. A crowded contact cell now yields the primary value and an advisory flag. Strictness that rejects valid humans is a bug in the rule, not in the data.

Why service terms are not on the customer

Cadence, rate, pool type and gate code are not facts about a person — they are the terms of what we promised. A billing-only QBO customer has none of them, so hanging them on Customer would give every customer fields that are meaningless for most of them.

The place itself is the other half of that line: ServiceLocation belongs to the customers domain — it is where this customer's pool is, and it exists whether or not anyone ever services it. Maintenance only references it by place id. Terms, on the other hand, belong to ServiceAgreement in the maintenance module, which also fixes the dependency direction: Maintenance may depend on Customers; Customers must never depend on Maintenance. With the terms on the customer, the onboarding pipeline was computing bi-weekly parities — the customer module knew what a pool was. It no longer does. The spine is lead → service agreement → task, and the agreement is exactly what Task.open() consumes.

Cadence, and why two days is usually a lie

ION renders a day picker only for weekly-class cadences. A bi-weekly or monthly task carries its day and its alternating-week parity inside one start date, so it can serve exactly one day. A second day in our cache is our own drift — an observed visit that slipped a day — and the ACL refuses to express it rather than inventing a schedule ION cannot hold.

Costly anchorOf() (date → weekday + parity) and startsOnFor() (weekday + parity → the first legal date) are exact inverses, and the selfcheck round-trips every weekday × parity × three anchors. They must also agree with the ingester's isoWeekParity, or Week A and Week B flip depending on which writer got there first.

Open questions

  • Where does the inbound flag live? customerFit is written for both doors, but nothing yet runs it over customers born directly in QBO. It wants a place to surface violations — most likely the data-quality view, keyed by account.
  • Should the link step be queued rather than invoked? The pieces are retriable and idempotent, so WORKFLOW_EXECUTION's queue + drainer would fit exactly; today it is a script Carter fires after running the sync.
  • Can the QBO→ION sync be triggered by us? If an endpoint exists it becomes one method on the Ion object and the awaiting window shrinks; nothing else in the design changes.
  • Where does ServiceAgreement live when it persists? It is currently a transient object between the source row and Task.open(). Once leads convert through it, it wants a row — and that row is also the natural home for the signup terms a customer service rep agrees on the phone.
  • Non-weekly day moves still refuse rather than rebasing the anchor. setStartDate exists and is proven; wiring it into the publish path is the remaining work.
