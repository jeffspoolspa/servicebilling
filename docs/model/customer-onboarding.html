Customer onboarding — two doors, one set of rules

A customer becomes ours in three moves that live on three different clocks: we validate and create (ours), QBO answers with an id (synchronous echo), and ION learns the customer exists (a sync we do not control). The model's core decision is that the waiting is a modelled state, not a null column — and that a task cannot be opened until it ends.

Ruled 2026-08-03 Everything we create flows through the factory, and everything always writes to QBO — but the factory is not the gate for existence. QBO is the leader and customers born directly in QuickBooks must be absorbed, validated, and flagged, never refused. So the rules live in one object (customerFit) that both doors call: the factory refuses on it before creating; the inbound path flags on it after the fact.

The core decision

Three facts forced the shape of this model, and each of them cost something to learn:

  1. The ION customer id does not exist at creation time. ION only learns of a customer when the QBO→ION sync (the ProEdge bridge) runs, and ION exposes no QBO key to look it up by (ADR 006). A promise in memory cannot survive that wait, so the wait is a state on the row.
  2. A service location's identity is its rooftop place id, not its street text. Normalized-street matching is a guess re-derived on every run; a place id is a fact resolved once. When the guessing machinery silently broke, a re-run created 65 duplicate accounts.
  3. Every external write is proven by an echo or a read-back, never by a status code. QBO returns the created Customer with its Id; ION is re-read after the POST.

Cadence, and why two days is usually a lie

ION renders a day picker only for weekly-class cadences. A bi-weekly or monthly task carries its day and its alternating-week parity inside one start date, so it can serve exactly one day. A second day in our cache is our own drift — an observed visit that slipped a day — and the ACL refuses to express it rather than inventing a schedule ION cannot hold.

Costly anchorOf() (date → weekday + parity) and startsOnFor() (weekday + parity → the first legal date) are exact inverses, and the selfcheck round-trips every weekday × parity × three anchors. They must also agree with the ingester's isoWeekParity, or Week A and Week B flip depending on which writer got there first.

Open questions

  • Where does the inbound flag live? customerFit is written for both doors, but nothing yet runs it over customers born directly in QBO. It wants a place to surface violations — most likely the data-quality view, keyed by account.
  • Should the link step be queued rather than invoked? The pieces are retriable and idempotent, so WORKFLOW_EXECUTION's queue + drainer would fit exactly; today it is a script Carter fires after running the sync.
  • Can the QBO→ION sync be triggered by us? If an endpoint exists it becomes one method on the Ion object and the awaiting window shrinks; nothing else in the design changes.
  • Non-weekly day moves still refuse rather than rebasing the anchor. setStartDate exists and is proven; wiring it into the publish path is the remaining work.
